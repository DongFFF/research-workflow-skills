# Design Mapping

Before prose expansion, fill one row per module:

| Module | Question | Input | Research task | Method and data | Validation | Output | Next use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concept definition | What is the construct? | Literature and actual cases | Define dimensions | Document analysis and exploratory cases | Concept comparison | Construct definitions | Measurement design |
| Mechanism study | How might the relationship work? | Construct definitions | Identify paths and boundaries | Feasible qualitative/quantitative design | Competing explanations | Mechanism model | Evaluation |
| Evaluation | Under what conditions does it hold? | Measures and model | Test the stated questions | Accessible observations and an appropriate method | Sensitivity/triangulation as warranted | Evidence with limitations | Application |
| Application | What decision can change? | Verified findings | Design and evaluate a tool/process | Scenario or field validation | User/implementation criteria | Practical output | Feedback |

This is a synthetic pattern, not a required four-module structure. Adapt to the actual proposal. Do not stack many methods without identifying each method's question and data requirements. A mechanism arrow in a plan indicates a proposed relationship until supported by evidence.

Distinguish scientific novelty from local relevance. A national scheme may emphasize general mechanisms, a municipal scheme may require a city decision interface, and a university scheme may foreground a local pilot while retaining theoretical scope. Read the call before choosing any of these emphases.

For each figure, record its caption, exact insertion paragraph, corresponding module, new information it conveys, editable source and preview filename. The overall figure should expose the full research chain; subfigures should explain each module rather than repeat it with different shapes.
