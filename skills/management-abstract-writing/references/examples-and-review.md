# Examples and decision worksheet

These are synthetic teaching fragments, not findings from an actual study. They deliberately omit sample statistics. Do not reuse their facts or recommendations unless the user's evidence supports them.

## The evidence-to-decision worksheet

Complete this reasoning privately before choosing the last sentence:

| Question | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| What did the study actually find? | An observed result rather than an aspiration or a plausible explanation |
| What distinction does that result establish? | For example, a resource stock differs from current deployable finance |
| Who faces the relevant choice? | A specific organizational actor or a defined research audience |
| What can that actor assess or decide? | Staffing, funding, sequencing, supplier evaluation, policy targeting or a testable theoretical inference |
| What is outside the evidence? | Untested interventions, causal compensation, population prevalence or observed environmental impact |

An implication should survive the question: **“What would this reader evaluate or decide differently?”** If the answer only restates “pay attention to sustainability,” the conclusion is still too abstract.

## Motivating multidimensional coordination

Weak:

> Digital investment is important for green development. This study uses two advanced methods.

Stronger, when supported:

> Firms coordinating technology renewal and environmental management must align objectives, personnel, operating routines and performance measures. Inconsistent priorities and uneven capabilities make it necessary to examine how conditions work together across organizational settings.

The improvement is a substantive coordination problem and a reason to study combinations. It is not a license to describe every construct as multidimensional.

## Selecting results and writing the ending

### Synthetic case A: organizational scale and project finance

Evidence supplied for the exercise: some high-readiness configurations include organizations with large asset bases and limited operating liquidity. The analysis is observational and does not establish financial substitution.

Weak ending:

> The findings enrich resource theory and provide important implications for management.

Decision-grounded ending:

> Managers can use the distinction between accumulated assets and operating liquidity to assess project funding separately from organizational scale when planning coordinated investments.

Do not conclude that external support “offsets” low cash flow or that organizations should reduce liquidity. Coexistence within a combination does not establish either claim.

### Synthetic case B: configurations and changing settings

Evidence supplied for the exercise: a pooled combination has uneven annual consistency; no organizational transitions or policy effects were identified.

Weak ending:

> The results demonstrate dynamic resilience and offer policy guidance.

Decision-grounded ending:

> Managers should periodically review whether a plan's staffing, funding and operating assumptions still fit the conditions under which it is being implemented.

This is an assessment implication, not proof that the review improves performance or that firms moved between configurations.

### Synthetic case C: institutional support

Evidence supplied for the exercise: public support appears in some favorable combinations and is unrestricted in others. There is no causal policy evaluation.

Decision-grounded ending:

> Policy evaluation should consider support alongside recipients' capabilities and financing conditions, with subsequent tests needed to establish the effects of particular support packages.

Do not infer that all firms require subsidies or that a specific subsidy amount is optimal.

- “开头和方法段保留，只改最后两句；明确企业管理者可以据此做什么决定。”
- “这个期刊有严格词数限制，请压缩程序细节，保留关键发现及证据边界。”

## Behavioral review cases

Use these as manual checks when changing the skill. They are not executable or phrase-matching tests.

1. **Correlation-only study:** supplied results are positive associations, with no identification strategy. A valid abstract retains associative language; it does not promise that increasing the predictor improves performance.
2. **Common but not necessary:** one condition appears in all solutions but fails the reported necessity test. A valid abstract distinguishes the two findings.
3. **Limited coverage:** favorable configurations have high consistency but cover a small share of outcome membership. A valid abstract avoids portraying them as an exhaustive typology.
4. **No managerial prescription:** the evidence establishes a measurement distinction with no tested intervention. A valid ending may recommend an assessment or state a specific theoretical inference; it must not invent actionable treatment effects.
5. **Accepted opening:** the user approves the background and methods and requests a new conclusion. A valid revision preserves those approved sections except for an explicitly needed word-limit adjustment.
6. **Short abstract:** under a tight limit, a valid revision keeps motivation, method rationale, essential evidence and a decision-grounded ending; it trims repetitive significance claims and procedural inventories first.
